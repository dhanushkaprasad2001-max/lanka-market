Cars, Motorbikes and Vehicles for Sale in Sri Lanka

Use this guide to compare vehicle adverts, prepare the right questions and carry out sensible checks before buying or selling a car, motorcycle, van or other vehicle.

A vehicle advert is only the starting point. The model, year and asking price matter, but documents, mechanical condition, ownership history and the seller's ability to complete a proper transfer matter just as much. VIKMA helps people discover advertisements; buyers and sellers should independently verify every important detail before making a payment.

Browse vehicle advertisements

Explore the approved advertisements available on VIKMA and contact the advertiser to confirm that the vehicle is still available. Listings can change as sellers update or remove adverts. Always arrange an in-person inspection and do not treat photographs or an online description as proof of condition or ownership.

Vehicle document checks in Sri Lanka

Before agreeing to buy, ask to see the original vehicle registration certificate (often called the CR book) and compare its details with the vehicle itself. Names, registration number, make, model, engine number and chassis number should be consistent. If anything is unclear, pause the transaction and seek independent advice.

  • Registration: Check the registration number and vehicle identity details against the documents and physical vehicle.
  • Ownership: Confirm who is legally entitled to sell the vehicle and whether the registered owner's details and seller's explanation match.
  • Finance or leasing: Ask whether any lease, loan, mortgage or other claim remains. Obtain reliable written confirmation of settlement and release where applicable.
  • Revenue licence and insurance: Check current documents and dates, and independently confirm what cover or permissions apply before driving.
  • Transfer: Understand the current ownership-transfer requirements and fees through the relevant Sri Lankan Department of Motor Traffic channels before paying.
  • Vehicle identity: Make sure the engine and chassis numbers have not been altered and match the registration documents.
Do not hand over the full purchase price based only on a photograph of a document, a promise to transfer later or a seller's claim that paperwork is being arranged.

How to inspect a used car or van

Inspect the vehicle in daylight, preferably when the engine is cold. If you are not confident assessing its condition, take an independent mechanic who has no connection to the seller. A short inspection can reveal warning signs, but it cannot guarantee that a vehicle will be trouble-free.

  • Look for uneven paint, mismatched body panels, rust, poor repairs and signs of accident or flood damage.
  • Check tyres for uneven wear and inspect visible suspension, lights, windows, seat belts and dashboard warning lights.
  • Start the engine and listen for unusual knocking, rattling, smoke or unstable idling.
  • Check for visible oil or coolant leaks and ask about recent repairs and scheduled maintenance.
  • During a suitable test drive, assess braking, steering, clutch or transmission, acceleration and unusual vibration.
  • Compare the odometer reading with available service records and the general wear of the vehicle. Mileage alone does not prove condition.

For a motorcycle, also inspect the frame, fork, wheels, chain or belt, brakes, tyres, lights, electrical system and cold-start behaviour. Ask for maintenance records and check that the engine and chassis identifiers match the registration documents.

Compare similar vehicles fairly

Do not compare asking prices without comparing the vehicles. Check the same make and model, model year, engine capacity, transmission, fuel type, mileage, trim or variant, condition and location. Consider whether the vehicle has documented servicing, replaced parts, accident history or outstanding repairs. A cheaper vehicle may need expensive work; a higher asking price is not proof of better condition.

Write down the questions you want answered and compare the seller's responses. Ask why the vehicle is being sold, how long the seller has owned it, whether it has been involved in an accident and whether there are known mechanical or electrical faults. Verify important claims independently.

What sellers should include in a vehicle advert

A complete and honest advert helps genuine buyers decide whether to make contact. Include:

  • Make, model, year of manufacture and model or trim variant.
  • Vehicle type, engine capacity, transmission and fuel type.
  • Current odometer reading and whether it can be supported by records.
  • Condition, service history, known faults and relevant repair or accident history.
  • Clear recent photographs of the front, rear, both sides, interior, dashboard and any visible damage.
  • District or town, asking price and a contact method you can respond to.
  • Accurate information about document status and any finance or transfer matter that a buyer should know.

Never hide a known fault or use photographs of a different vehicle. Update or remove an advert when the vehicle is sold, no longer available or the price changes.

Safer contact, viewing and payment

Arrange to view the vehicle in a sensible public place and, where possible, bring another person. Verify the seller's identity and authority to sell. Do not share one-time passwords, banking PINs or account passwords. Be cautious of urgent pressure, unusually low prices, requests to pay before viewing, fake courier arrangements or deposits to an unverified account.

Agree in writing on the vehicle, price, included items, document status and the steps for payment and ownership transfer. Use a traceable payment method and keep receipts and copies of relevant signed documents. Read VIKMA's safety guidance before arranging a transaction.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check first when buying a used vehicle?

Start by matching the registration and vehicle identity details, confirming the seller's authority, and arranging an independent mechanical inspection. Do not pay before you are satisfied with the documents and condition.

Is a low-mileage vehicle always a better purchase?

No. Mileage is only one factor. Service history, age, maintenance, usage, accident history and current mechanical condition all matter.

Can I rely on the photographs in an advert?

No. Photographs help you shortlist vehicles, but they cannot confirm ownership, hidden damage or mechanical condition. Inspect the actual vehicle and documents.

What information should I put in my vehicle advert?

Include the make, model, year, mileage, engine capacity, transmission, fuel type, condition, known faults, location, price and clear current photographs.

Does VIKMA guarantee a vehicle or seller?

No. VIKMA is a classifieds platform for discovering advertisements. Buyers and sellers are responsible for checking details, documents, condition and transaction arrangements independently.
